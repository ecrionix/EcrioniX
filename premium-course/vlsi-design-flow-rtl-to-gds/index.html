VLSI Design Flow: RTL to GDS

Understand the complete ASIC implementation journey, from RTL design to final GDSII generation. Explore architectural abstractions, logic synthesis, static timing analysis (STA), DFT, floorplanning, placement, clock tree synthesis, routing, and physical verification signoff.

Level: Beginner–Intermediate
Category: VLSI / ASIC Design
Pace: Self-paced
Prerequisites: Basic Digital Logic
▶ Start Learning (Lesson 01) 📑 View Curriculum

🎯 What You Will Learn

✓ Complete RTL-to-GDS Flow: Understand each stage of the ASIC journey from architecture to silicon fabrication.
✓ RTL Design & Verification: Master synthesizable Verilog HDL constructs and functional verification testbenches.
✓ Logic Synthesis & Optimization: Translate behavioral RTL into gate-level netlists with technology cell mapping.
✓ Static Timing Analysis (STA): Formulate SDC constraints, calculate path delays, and resolve setup/hold violations.
✓ Floorplanning & Power Planning: Define core aspect ratios, allocate macro positions, and design robust power delivery networks.
✓ Placement & Clock Tree Synthesis: Place standard cells, balance clock skew across domains, and optimize latency.
✓ Routing, Physical Signoff & GDSII: Perform detailed routing, extraction (SPEF), DRC/LVS physical checks, and tape-out export.

ℹ️ Course Information & Prerequisites

Course Overview

This curriculum bridges front-end logic architecture with back-end physical implementation. It demystifies the entire semiconductor engineering pipeline so engineers understand how RTL code becomes functional silicon in a modern foundry.

Prerequisites

  • Basic boolean algebra and digital logic design (gates, flip-flops, muxes).
  • Fundamental electronics principles (transistors, voltage, current).
  • No prior commercial EDA tool licenses required.

Skills Covered

  • Verilog RTL modeling & testbenches
  • Synopsys Design Constraints (SDC) formulation
  • Static Timing Analysis (STA) setup/hold slack
  • Floorplanning, placement & clock tree balancing
  • Physical verification: DRC, LVS & GDSII signoff

Intended Audience

  • Electrical & Computer Engineering (ECE/EEE) students
  • RTL design engineers seeking physical flow awareness
  • Physical design engineers seeking deeper architectural context
  • Hardware professionals preparing for semiconductor interviews

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is this course completely free to access? ▼
Yes. EcrioniX is dedicated to democratizing advanced semiconductor engineering education. All modules, study guides, diagrams, and code artifacts are freely accessible online without any paywalls or subscriptions.
How are new modules and lessons published? ▼
The course follows a progressive publication model. Lesson 1.1 is available now, and subsequent lessons and modules are released as thoroughly vetted, technically verified chapters complete with industry-standard diagrams and RTL examples.
Do I need proprietary EDA software (e.g., Synopsys, Cadence) to follow along? ▼
No. While the course teaches industry-standard methodologies used in commercial tools, all principles are demonstrated using accessible concepts, open-source flows (such as OpenROAD, Yosys, and OpenSTA), and clear diagrams that require no licensed software.
How does this course connect with other EcrioniX resources? ▼
This course integrates directly with our interactive tools—including the Silicon Explorer, Digital Timing Waveform Generator, and the RTL Practice Problems catalog—allowing you to reinforce theoretical lessons with hands-on practice.
