Lesson 10.9

Interview Problems: "Fix this path"

In almost every physical design or STA interview, you will be handed a piece of paper with a timing path and asked, "How would you fix this?"

1

Problem 1: The Giant Setup Violation

Scenario: You have a setup violation of -800ps on a 1ns clock (1 GHz). The data path has 30 levels of logic.

The Answer

Do NOT say "upsize the cells". 30 levels of logic cannot run at 1 GHz no matter how much you upsize. The answer is: "We need to pipeline the RTL. I will go to the RTL designer and ask them to insert flops to break up the combinational cloud."

2

Problem 2: The Impossible Hold Violation

Scenario: You have a hold violation between Flop A and Flop B. You added 10 delay buffers, but the violation is still there. Why?

The Answer

This is a trick question regarding Clock Skew. The clock tree to Flop B must be extremely delayed compared to Flop A. Adding data buffers just treats the symptom. "I need to look at the clock tree and fix the massive skew on the capture clock path, or check if they are actually in different clock domains requiring a synchronizer."

3

Problem 3: Setup AND Hold Failure on the Same Path

Scenario: A path fails both setup and hold at the same time.

The Answer

This happens due to excessive OCV (On-Chip Variation) / derating or massive crosstalk delta-delay where the min/max window is wider than the clock period. "I would reduce pessimism. I'd enable CRPR (Clock Reconvergence Pessimism Removal), enable PBA (Path-Based Analysis), or fix the SI crosstalk aggressors."

🎯 Interview Tips

  • Always ask clarifying questions (What node? What frequency? Is this post-route?).
  • Think structurally (RTL changes, clock tree) before suggesting cell resizing.
