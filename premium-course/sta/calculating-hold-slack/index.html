Calculating Hold Slack

Welcome to Module 2, Lesson 3 of the EcrioniX STA Premium Course. In this lesson, we will dive deep into hold timing analysis. While setup analysis ensures that data is fast enough to be captured on the next clock edge, hold analysis ensures that data is slow enough to not overwrite the current data being captured.

The Fundamental Hold Timing Equation

Hold time (Thold) is the minimum amount of time that data must remain stable after the active clock edge at the capturing flip-flop. If the data changes too quickly after the clock edge, the capturing flip-flop may capture the new data instead of the old data, or it might go into metastability.

To prevent a hold violation, the fastest path from the launching flip-flop (FF1) to the capturing flip-flop (FF2) must take longer than the hold time requirement of FF2.

Tcq + Tcomb_min ≥ Thold

Where:

  • Tcq: Clock-to-Q delay of the launching flip-flop (FF1).
  • Tcomb_min: The minimum (fastest) combinational logic delay between FF1 and FF2.
  • Thold: The hold time requirement of the capturing flip-flop (FF2).
Why min delay? Hold violations happen when data races through the logic too quickly. Therefore, we must evaluate the fastest possible path (best-case or min delay) to ensure it's still slow enough to satisfy the hold requirement.
Independence from Clock Frequency

A crucial and often-tested concept in STA is that hold time analysis is independent of the clock frequency (or clock period), assuming a zero-skew clock network.

Let's look at why this is true:

  • Setup Analysis: Data launched on Edge 0 must arrive before Edge 1. The time between Edge 0 and Edge 1 is the clock period (T). Thus, setup slack depends heavily on T.
  • Hold Analysis: Data launched on Edge 0 must not interfere with the data that was just captured on Edge 0. Both the launch and the capture check occur relative to the exact same clock edge.

Because the launch and capture events are evaluated at the same instant in time (Edge 0), the time elapsed between clock edges (the period) simply cancels out of the equation. No matter if your clock is 10 MHz or 1 GHz, if you have a hold violation, you cannot fix it by slowing down the clock.

Hold Data Arrival Time vs Hold Data Required Time

Just like with setup time, we calculate Slack by comparing the Data Arrival Time (DAT) with the Data Required Time (DRT). However, the slack equation is slightly different for hold.

Hold Data Arrival Time (DAT)

This is how fast the new data actually arrives at the D pin of the capturing flop.

Hold DAT = Launch Clock Delay + T_cq + T_comb_min

Hold Data Required Time (DRT)

This is how long the old data needs to stay stable at the D pin. The data must remain stable until the capture clock edge arrives, plus the hold time of the flop.

Hold DRT = Capture Clock Delay + T_hold

Hold Slack Equation

For hold timing, the data must arrive after it is required. Therefore, Arrival Time must be greater than Required Time.

Hold Slack = Hold DAT - Hold DRT

If Hold Slack ≥ 0, the timing is met. If Hold Slack < 0, there is a hold violation (data arrived too early).

Worked Example

Let's calculate the hold slack for a simple path between two flip-flops, FF1 and FF2.

Parameter Value Description
Clock Period (T) 10 ns (Included to show it doesn't matter)
Launch Clock Network Delay 1.2 ns Delay from clock source to FF1 CK pin
Capture Clock Network Delay 1.5 ns Delay from clock source to FF2 CK pin
Tcq 0.8 ns Clock-to-Q delay of FF1
Tcomb_min 0.5 ns Fastest path through combinational logic
Thold 0.3 ns Hold time requirement of FF2

Step 1: Calculate Hold Data Arrival Time (DAT)

Hold DAT = Launch Clock Delay + Tcq + Tcomb_min

Hold DAT = 1.2 ns + 0.8 ns + 0.5 ns = 2.5 ns

Interpretation: The new data will arrive at the D pin of FF2 at 2.5 ns absolute time.

Step 2: Calculate Hold Data Required Time (DRT)

Hold DRT = Capture Clock Delay + Thold

Hold DRT = 1.5 ns + 0.3 ns = 1.8 ns

Interpretation: The old data must remain stable at the D pin of FF2 until 1.8 ns absolute time.

Step 3: Calculate Hold Slack

Hold Slack = Hold DAT - Hold DRT

Hold Slack = 2.5 ns - 1.8 ns = +0.7 ns

Result: Since the Hold Slack is positive (+0.7 ns), there is no hold violation. The data arrived at 2.5ns, well after the required time of 1.8ns.

Notice that the clock skew (Capture Delay - Launch Delay) is 1.5 - 1.2 = +0.3 ns. Positive skew makes hold timing harder to meet, as it delays the capture clock and thus delays when the data is allowed to change. In this case, our combinational logic and Tcq were slow enough to absorb this skew.
