Lesson 5.2

The Hold Slack Equation

While setup makes sure data arrives early enough, hold makes sure data stays long enough. Data must not change too quickly after the clock edge, or it might corrupt the capture process.

1

The Core Principle

Data launched by a clock edge must remain stable at the destination flip-flop for a brief window after the capture clock edge. Unlike setup, hold checks happen on the same clock edge for both launch and capture.

Data Arrival Time (DAT)

The earliest time new data can arrive at the capture flop.
DAT = Launch Clock Path + Clock-to-Q Delay (min) + Datapath Delay (min)

Data Required Time (DRT)

The time until which the old data must remain stable.
DRT = Capture Clock Path + Hold Time

2

Hold Slack

For hold, data must arrive after it is required to be held.

Hold Slack = Data Arrival Time (DAT) - Data Required Time (DRT)

Notice the order is swapped compared to setup! Positive slack means the data arrives later than the hold time requirement, meaning the old data was held safely. Negative slack means a shoot-through violation.

Data must remain stable throughout the hold window.

Hold is independent of frequency! Since launch and capture are on the same edge, the clock period cancels out. You cannot fix hold violations by slowing down the clock.

🎯 What to remember

  • Hold checks prevent "shoot-through" where data races through logic too fast.
  • Hold slack is independent of clock period.
  • Fixed by adding delay buffers in the datapath.
