STA / Module 6: Delay Modeling
Lesson 6.9

Temperature Inversion

Normally, electronics run slower when they get hot. But in deep sub-micron nodes (below 65nm), an effect called Temperature Inversion turns this intuition upside down.

1

The Classic View

In older nodes, higher temperature means higher carrier scattering (lower mobility). Lower mobility equals lower drive current (Ids), which equals higher delay. Thus, Maximum Delay (setup checks) was always run at the Highest Temperature (e.g., 125C).

2

The Sub-Micron Reality

As VDD scales down in advanced nodes (e.g., 0.8V or 0.6V), the transistor threshold voltage (Vth) scaling becomes critical. As temperature increases, Vth DECREASES. A lower Vth increases the drive current (Ids).

Temperature Inversion

At very low operating voltages, the increase in current due to Vth lowering dominates the decrease in current due to mobility degradation. As a result, the transistor runs FASTER at high temperatures and SLOWER at low temperatures.

Impact on STA: We can no longer assume that Max Delay occurs at Max Temp. STA must be performed across multiple temperature corners (e.g., -40C, 25C, 125C) to guarantee timing closure.
