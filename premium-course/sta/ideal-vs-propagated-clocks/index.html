Lesson 4.1

Ideal vs. Propagated Clocks

Before Clock Tree Synthesis (CTS), clock networks are ideal. After CTS, they are propagated. Understanding this transition is crucial for timing closure.

1

Ideal Clocks

During RTL synthesis and early placement, the clock tree does not exist. The STA tool assumes the clock reaches every flip-flop instantaneously (zero delay) or with a fixed user-defined latency.

Definition

An ideal clock has no physical RC delays. Setup and hold checks rely on estimated timing (clock uncertainty) rather than actual buffer delays.

2

Propagated Clocks

After CTS, actual clock buffers and routing metal are added. The tool traces the exact physical path from the clock root to each sink.

Once a clock is set to propagated, STA uses the actual delay of the clock tree cells and nets to compute insertion delay and skew.

3

Transition in the Flow

The command set_propagated_clock [all_clocks] marks the shift. Ideal clocks hide hold violations; propagated clocks expose the harsh reality of clock skew.
