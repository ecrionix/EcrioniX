Lesson 3.8

Drive Strength & Output Load

Timing isn't just about logic delays. In the real world, the electrical load on a port physically slows down the signal transition. STA must model this physics.

1

Setting Input Transition (Drive Strength)

When an external signal arrives at your input port, the STA tool needs to know how "sharp" or "sluggish" the waveform edge is. A slow transition adds delay to the first gate inside your block.

SDC Command: set_input_transition
# Tell STA the input waveform takes 0.5ns to rise/fall
set_input_transition 0.5 [get_ports data_in]
Driving a car analogy

Drive strength (transition) is like horsepower. A weak drive strength means a slow, sluggish start. The first gate inside your block feels that sluggishness.

2

Setting Output Load (Capacitance)

When your logic drives an output port, it has to charge up the external wire. Higher capacitance (load) means the output transition will be slower, increasing delay.

SDC Command: set_load
# Model the external wire and receiving gate as a 10pF capacitor
set_load 10.0 [get_ports data_out]

If you don't define an output load, the STA tool assumes 0 capacitance. The synthesis tool will use a tiny, weak gate to drive the port. When hooked up in the real chip, that gate will fail to drive the heavy external wire, causing massive timing violations.

🎯 What to remember

  • `set_input_transition` models the sharpness of the incoming signal edge.
  • `set_load` models the electrical weight (capacitance) on the output port.
  • Without these, synthesis will optimize gates incorrectly for the block boundary.
