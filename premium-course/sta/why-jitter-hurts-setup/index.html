Lesson 5.4

Why Jitter Hurts Setup but Not Hold

Clock jitter is the random variation in the clock period from a PLL. It eats into your timing budget, but only for setup.

1

Jitter in Setup Checks

Setup checks compare the launch edge to the next clock edge. Because these are two different edges generated by the PLL separated by one clock cycle, the exact time between them can vary due to jitter.

STA tools subtract the uncertainty (jitter) from the Required Time, making the setup check strictly harder.

2

Jitter in Hold Checks

Hold checks compare data launched and captured on the exact same clock edge. Since it is the physical same edge arriving at two different flops, the variation in the PLL's period does not matter.

If the edge arrives 10ps early due to jitter, it arrives 10ps early for both the launch and capture flops. The relative difference (skew) remains unaffected by cycle-to-cycle jitter.

🎯 What to remember

  • Setup checks span two different clock edges, so jitter shrinks the clock period.
  • Hold checks happen on the same clock edge, meaning jitter cancels itself out.
