Lesson 3.1

Path 1: Reg-to-Reg (The standard path)

The most common path in any digital design is from one flip-flop to another. The Reg-to-Reg path is fully constrained internally, making it the most well-behaved path in STA.

1

Anatomy of a Reg-to-Reg Path

A register-to-register path (often called an internal path) starts at the clock pin of a launch flip-flop and ends at the data pin of a capture flip-flop.

  • Startpoint: Clock pin (CP) of the launching flip-flop.
  • Endpoint: Data input pin (D) of the capturing flip-flop.
  • Path contents: Clock-to-Q delay of the launch flop + delay of combinational logic + routing delay.

Signal traversing a Reg-to-Reg path across one clock cycle.

Because both the launch and capture flops are inside your design and clocked by defined clocks, the STA tool knows exactly how much time is available (1 clock cycle). You do not need to apply external delay constraints to these paths.

2

Setup and Hold Checks on Internal Paths

STA automatically calculates the required times for setup and hold based on the clock period.

Setup Check

Data must arrive at the capture flop's D pin before the next clock edge minus the library setup time.

Hold Check

Data must remain stable at the capture flop's D pin after the capturing clock edge for at least the library hold time.

🎯 What to remember

  • Reg-to-Reg paths are fully internal and bounded by your clock period.
  • They start at a flop's clock pin and end at the next flop's data pin.
  • You constrain these simply by defining the clock (`create_clock`).
