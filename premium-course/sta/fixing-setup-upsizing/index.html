Lesson 9.1

Fixing Setup: Cell Upsizing

The simplest and most common way to fix a setup violation is to upsize the cells driving the critical path. Bigger cells mean stronger drive strength, which reduces delay.

1

Why Upsizing Works

Setup time violations occur because the data takes too long to reach the destination flip-flop. The delay of a logic gate depends primarily on its output load (capacitance) and its internal drive strength (size of its transistors).

Core Principle

Upsizing increases the width of the transistors in the standard cell. This lowers the output resistance (R), reducing the RC time constant and making the transition (slew) faster.

🚗 Analogy

Think of standard cells as water pumps and the wire capacitance as a swimming pool. If it takes too long to fill the pool (delay), you replace your small pump (X1 drive strength) with an industrial pump (X4 drive strength). The pool fills much faster.

2

The Trade-offs of Upsizing

If bigger cells are faster, why don't we just make every cell huge from the start? Because upsizing comes with severe penalties:

  • Area: Larger cells take up more silicon real estate.
  • Power: Larger transistors have higher leakage and higher dynamic switching power.
  • Input Capacitance: A larger cell presents a higher load to the cell driving it. Upsizing a cell speeds up its own output, but slows down the previous stage!

The Domino Effect: If you upsize Cell B to drive Cell C faster, the input capacitance of Cell B increases. Now, Cell A (which drives Cell B) has a heavier load and slows down. You must ensure that the net gain on the path is positive.

3

When to use it

Upsizing is the first tool synthesis and PnR (Place and Route) tools reach for when fixing setup. As an STA engineer doing ECOs (Engineering Change Orders), you look for:

  • Cells on the critical path with poor slew (transition time).
  • Cells driving large fan-outs or long nets with weak drive strengths (e.g., an X1 or X2 cell driving a large load).

ECO Example: `size_cell u_logic_and2 AND2X1 AND2X4`
