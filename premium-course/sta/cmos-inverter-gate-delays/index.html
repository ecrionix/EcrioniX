Lesson 1.1

The CMOS Inverter & Gate Delays

Before calculating path delays, you must understand the fundamental building block of all digital logic: the CMOS inverter. It is the atom of STA.

1

Anatomy of an Inverter

A CMOS inverter consists of a PMOS pull-up transistor and an NMOS pull-down transistor. When the input is HIGH, the NMOS turns on, pulling the output LOW. When the input is LOW, the PMOS turns on, pulling the output HIGH.

Delay Origins

Delay is fundamentally the time it takes to charge or discharge the parasitic capacitance at a node through the effective resistance of the transistors.

2

RC Delay Model

The time it takes for a signal to transition is governed by tau = R * C. A larger load capacitance (C) or a weaker drive strength (higher R) increases the delay.

Water Bucket Analogy

Think of capacitance as a bucket, and resistance as the hose pipe's narrowness. Delay is the time to fill or empty the bucket. A huge bucket (high fanout load) takes longer to fill. A thin hose (weak transistor) takes longer to pump water.

🎯 What to remember

  • Gate delay depends on input transition time (slew) and output load capacitance.
  • Bigger transistors reduce R (speeding up transitions) but increase input capacitance.
