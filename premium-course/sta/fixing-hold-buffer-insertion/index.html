Lesson 9.5

Fixing Hold: Buffer Insertion

A hold violation means data is traveling too fast. The standard and most reliable way to slow it down is through buffer insertion (adding delay).

1

Why Add Delay?

Hold time checks ensure that the new data does not arrive so fast that it corrupts the capture of the old data on the current clock edge. If a path is too fast (typically a direct flop-to-flop path with no logic in between), it fails the hold check.

The Solution

We intentionally add delay elements (buffers or pairs of inverters) into the data path. This increases the propagation delay without changing the logical function.

2

ECO Considerations

In the final stages of a chip design (ECO phase), hold fixing is the primary focus. Why?

Frequency Independence: Hold violations are independent of clock frequency. You cannot fix a hold violation post-silicon by slowing down the clock. A hold violation means a dead chip. Setup violations can be bypassed by running the clock slower, but hold violations are fatal.

When inserting buffers to fix hold, we must be careful not to add too much delay, or we will create a setup violation on the same path! This is known as closing the timing window.
