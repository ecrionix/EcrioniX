STA / Module 5: Setup & Hold Math
Lesson 5.7

Half-Cycle Paths (Negedge sampling)

What happens when data is launched on the rising edge and captured on the falling edge? Your timing budget just got cut in half.

1

Setup Check on Half-Cycle

If launch is posedge and capture is negedge, the next capturing edge is exactly half a clock cycle away.

Required Time = (Period / 2) - Setup Time

This path requires extremely fast combinatorial logic. It's often used in high-speed DDR (Double Data Rate) interfaces where you sample on both edges.

2

Hold Check on Half-Cycle

Hold checks always occur against the capture edge prior to the setup capture edge. For a half-cycle path, the prior negedge is exactly half a cycle before the launch edge.

Wait, before the launch edge? Yes. STA will check hold against the negedge at t = -0.5 cycles. Because data cannot travel back in time, half-cycle paths typically have massive, positive hold slack.

Data has only half a clock period to stabilize.

🎯 What to remember

  • Half-cycle paths cut the setup time budget in half.
  • They make hold checks trivially easy to meet.
  • Avoid them internally; use them mostly for off-chip DDR interfaces.
