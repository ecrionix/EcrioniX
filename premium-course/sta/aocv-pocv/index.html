Lesson 7.6

Advanced OCV (AOCV) / Parametric OCV (POCV)

Traditional OCV applies a flat penalty (e.g., 10%) to all paths. In advanced nodes (< 28nm), this is overly pessimistic and causes tools to add unnecessary buffers, killing power and area. AOCV and POCV introduce statistical intelligence to variation modeling.

1

Advanced OCV (AOCV)

AOCV uses logic depth (number of gates in a path) and physical distance to calculate derating factors. It relies on the statistical principle of Cancellation.

The Law of Large Numbers

If a path has 50 gates, it is highly improbable that ALL 50 gates are exceptionally slow. Variations tend to cancel out (one slow gate is offset by a fast gate). AOCV applies smaller derating factors to deep logic paths, reducing pessimism.

2

Parametric OCV (POCV / SOCV)

Even AOCV struggles at 7nm and below. POCV (also called SOCV) ditches derate tables entirely. Instead, every cell delay is modeled as a statistical distribution (Mean + Sigma). Delay calculation becomes a statistical summation of distributions, not absolute numbers.

pocv_math.txt
Total Delay = μ_total ± N * √(σ1² + σ2² + ... + σn²)

Because variances add in quadrature, the standard deviation of a long path grows much slower than linear addition. This natively removes pessimism.

POCV requires specific Liberty Variation Format (LVF) models from the foundry, detailing the sigma values for every arc in the standard cell library.

🎯 Key Takeaway

  • Flat OCV over-penalizes deep logic paths.
  • AOCV uses lookup tables based on path depth and distance.
  • POCV models delays as statistical distributions (Mean, Sigma) to mathematically eliminate pessimism.
