Lesson 9.6

Fixing Hold: Downsizing

If adding a buffer costs area, we can slow data down by downsizing cells instead. Smaller cells drive slower, naturally fixing hold violations.

1

The Free Fix

Unlike inserting a buffer (which increases the total die area), downsizing a cell (e.g., from an AND2X4 to an AND2X1) actually saves area and reduces leakage power.

Dual Benefit

Downsizing makes the driving transistor weaker. This increases the delay through the cell (fixing hold), whilst simultaneously recovering silicon area and lowering dynamic/leakage power consumption.

2

The Catch: Transition Slew

We cannot just aggressively downsize everything. A very small cell driving a large load will result in a very slow transition (slew rate).

Max Transition Limits: Foundries specify strict max transition rules (e.g., no signal can take longer than 1.5ns to rise). If you downsize a cell too much, you will create a max transition violation, and a slow rising edge is highly susceptible to noise and crosstalk.
