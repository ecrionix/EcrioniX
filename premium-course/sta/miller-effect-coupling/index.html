Lesson 6.10

Miller Effect & Coupling Capacitance

Because wires run closely parallel to each other, they interact. The Miller Effect describes how coupling capacitance dynamically changes based on switching activity.

1

Coupling Capacitance (Cc)

A wire doesn't just have capacitance to ground (Cg); it has capacitance to neighboring wires (Cc). In advanced nodes, wires are taller and closer together, making Cc dominate total capacitance (often >70% of total C).

2

The Miller Effect

The effective capacitance seen by a driver depends on whether the neighboring net (aggressor) is switching at the same time as the primary net (victim).

Opposite Switching (Worst Case)

If the victim transitions 0→1 while the aggressor transitions 1→0, the voltage change across the capacitor is 2×VDD. The effective coupling capacitance becomes 2 × Cc. Delay increases significantly.

Same Switching (Best Case)

If both victim and aggressor transition 0→1 together, there is no voltage change across the capacitor. The effective coupling capacitance is 0. Delay decreases.

Crosstalk Delay Analysis

STA tools use Signal Integrity (SI) analysis to identify timing windows. If an aggressor can switch during the victim's transition window, the tool applies the Miller multiplier (bump) to calculate the worst-case (or best-case) delay.
