Lesson 7.10

Derating Factors

To account for inaccuracies in models, extraction tools, and environmental unknowns, STA engineers manually insert Derating Factors as a final safety margin before tapeout.

1

What is a Derate?

A derate is a scalar multiplier applied to calculated delays in STA. It is applied using commands like set_timing_derate.

derate_command.tcl
# Apply 10% pessimism to late (setup launch) paths
set_timing_derate -late -data 1.10

# Apply 5% optimism to early (setup capture) paths
set_timing_derate -early -clock 0.95
2

Why do we need them?

  • Aging / BTI: Over 10 years, transistors degrade (Bias Temperature Instability) and become slower. A derate ensures the chip still meets timing in year 10.
  • PLL Jitter / Clock Uncertainty: Clocks aren't perfectly periodic. Derates account for cycle-to-cycle jitter.
  • Tool inaccuracy: RC extractors have a +/- 5% margin of error compared to actual silicon.

Derating is a blunt instrument. Over-derating leads to unroutable designs with impossible timing constraints. This is why the industry shifted to AOCV and POCV (to model variation statistically rather than bluntly).

🎯 Key Takeaway

  • Derates add synthetic pessimism to STA to cover unknowns like aging, jitter, and tool error.
  • Late paths are multiplied by > 1.0 (made slower).
  • Early paths are multiplied by < 1.0 (made faster).
  • This concludes Module 7. You now understand exactly how the physical environment impacts timing analysis!
