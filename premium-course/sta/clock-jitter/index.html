Lesson 4.3

Clock Jitter (Deterministic vs. Random)

Jitter is the short-term variation of a digital signal's significant instants from their ideal positions in time.

1

Deterministic Jitter (DJ)

DJ is bounded and predictable. Sources include:

  • Power supply noise (SSN)
  • Crosstalk from adjacent signals
  • Duty cycle distortion
2

Random Jitter (RJ)

RJ is unbounded and typically follows a Gaussian distribution. It stems from fundamental physical phenomena like thermal noise in the PLL oscillator.

3

Modeling in STA

STA handles jitter via the set_clock_uncertainty command. Since RJ is unbounded, we use a statistical peak-to-peak value (like 14-sigma) for timing signoff.
