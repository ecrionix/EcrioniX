RTL Design / Module 1: Complete VLSI Flow
Lesson 1.9

Sign-off & tape-out — STA, power, DRC/LVS, what “done” means

Tape-out is the moment design data is released for manufacturing. Before that, sign-off checks argue the chip is ready — timing, power, and physical correctness.

1

Major sign-off families

STA+ Power+ DRC+ LVS→ Tape-out→ Foundry

Tape-out is a release decision after sign-off evidence — not a vibe.

⏱️

STA

Static Timing Analysis — prove timing without needing all vectors.

⚡

Power

IR drop, peaks, leakage estimates — will the grid survive?

📐

DRC

Design Rule Checks — geometry obeys foundry rules.

🔗

LVS

Layout vs Schematic — layout matches intended connectivity.

Each sign-off type — deeper

CheckWhat it provesTypical fail symptom
STA (setup)Data arrives before capture edgeNegative setup slack on critical path
STA (hold)Data stable after capture edgeHold violation after CTS skew change
IR dropSupply voltage within spec under loadLogic slows or fails at high activity
EM / thermalCurrent density and heat OKLong-term reliability risk
DRCGeometry rules (width, spacing)Shorts, opens at manufacturing
LVSLayout netlist = schematic netlistMissing via, wrong device count
2

What “done” is (and is not)

  • Done means accepted risk is documented and residual issues are owned.
  • Not done means “it boots in the FPGA sometimes” with no reports.

Even after tape-out, silicon bring-up can find surprises. Sign-off reduces risk — it does not grant magic immunity.

Risk acceptance

Ship decisions are not “zero bugs” — they are documented residual risk:

  • Known timing waiver on non-critical path with owner sign-off
  • DV coverage hole accepted for v1 with software workaround
  • Formal sign-off deck archived for audit
⚠ Trap

“We’ll fix it in software” without a ticket and owner — that is how recall programs start.

ECO pain story

A hold violation found one week before tape-out may need a metal-only ECO — moving cells, inserting buffers, no RTL change if lucky. An RTL FSM bug found then may cost millions and months for mask respin. That is why Module 1 exists.

3

Why RTL engineers should care

  • Late RTL ECOs during sign-off are painful and expensive
  • Clean CDC and timing-friendly RTL reduce last-minute fires
  • Understanding reports helps you talk with PD/STA teammates
💡 Airplane checklist analogy

Pilots don’t skip the checklist because the last flight was fine. Sign-off is the checklist before you leave the runway toward the foundry.

RTL engineers often read STA report lines like this (simplified):

timing_report_excerpt.txt
Path Group: clk_sys
Startpoint: u_i2c/byte_fsm/state_reg[0] (rising edge-triggered FF)
Endpoint:   u_i2c/bit_engine/scl_oe_reg   (rising edge-triggered FF)
Clock:      clk_sys (period 10.000ns)

  data arrival time ............  8.420 ns
  required time ................  9.500 ns
  slack (MET) ..................  1.080 ns

# Negative slack = FAIL (must fix via RTL, constraints, or PD)
# Your early RTL choices decide how hard this fight becomes

Reading reports — practice questions

  • Is slack positive or negative?
  • Which clock domain and path group?
  • Launch vs capture flop — can you find them in RTL?
  • Is failure setup or hold? (fix differs)
4

FPGA vs ASIC sign-off

ASIC sign-offFPGA sign-off
STA, DRC, LVS, IR dropPost-PAR timing report
Foundry GDSII handoffBitstream generation
ATE + scan at factoryBoard functional test
ECO / respin costlyReprogram in minutes

FPGA “sign-off” looks like timing reports + successful bitstream + board bring-up. Different artifacts, same seriousness about timing and verification.

💼 Interview angle

“What is tape-out?” — The release of final GDSII (layout) to the foundry for mask fabrication — after all sign-off checks pass or risks are accepted.

Sign-off vocabulary

TermMeaning

🎯 What to remember

  • Tape-out releases manufacturing data after sign-off.
  • STA, power, DRC, and LVS are core vocabulary.
  • RTL quality early prevents ECO panic late.
