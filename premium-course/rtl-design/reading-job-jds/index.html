RTL Design / Module 12: Career & Interview
Lesson 12.3 · Job market

Reading job JDs — ASIC vs FPGA vs IP design roles

Job descriptions are wish lists, not exams. Your job is to decode must-have vs nice-to-have, spot red flags, and map what you actually built in this course to the bullets recruiters keyword-scan for.

JD reading rule

If you match 60% of must-haves and can speak honestly to 2 portfolio artifacts, apply. Don't self-reject on nice-to-haves like "5 years UVM" when you're targeting junior RTL.

3

Red flags in job postings

Red flag 1

"Full-stack chip engineer" — RTL + PD + verification + analog for one person. Often understaffed team or unrealistic scope.

Red flag 2

"Expert in everything" — 15 must-haves including UVM, STA, FPGA, and embedded C. Real role is probably one of those.

Red flag 3

No team / product context — Generic copy-paste JD with no chip name, no block description, no tech node hint.

Red flag 4

"Unpaid trial project" — Legitimate companies don't ask for weeks of free RTL work upfront.

Red flag 5

Salary range missing + "competitive" only — Not always bad, but pair with Glassdoor/levels research before investing interview prep.

Green flags

Named block (e.g. "DDR controller," "PCIe PHY wrapper"), specific tools, mention of mentorship, clear junior/mid/senior level, realistic tool list matching the role family.

Sample JD annotations — 3 fictional postings

JD A — Junior ASIC RTL Engineer (Sensor IoT startup)

Requirements: Verilog RTL for peripheral blocks. FSM and CSR design. Lint-clean code. Simulation with directed tests. Understanding of setup/hold. CDC awareness. Nice: SystemVerilog, UVM exposure.

✓ Strong match. Lead with I2C capstone. Must-haves: Verilog, FSM, CSR, sim, lint — all covered. Nice UVM: say "directed TB today; UVM on roadmap." Apply.
JD B — FPGA Design Engineer (Defense contractor)

Requirements: Xilinx Vivado, Verilog, timing constraints, on-chip debug (ILA), board bring-up. 3+ years. AXI interconnect experience required.

△ Partial match. RTL skills transfer; tool-specific gap (Vivado/ILA). "3+ years" may be flexible with portfolio. Learn constraint basics before interview. Mention I2C review SDC excerpt.
JD C — Senior IP Design Lead (Established semiconductor)

Requirements: 10+ years, ownership of high-speed SerDes IP, timing closure to 2 GHz, UVM regression ownership, customer support.

✗ Poor match for new grads. Senior + SerDes + 10 years = skip unless internal transfer. Good for calibrating what "senior" looks like.

Resume trap

Interview honesty

If the JD says "verification" in the title but lists UVM as must-have, that's a DV role — your I2C TB is relevant, but lead with "directed self-checking" not "designed the RTL" unless the bullet fits.

13

Quick JD triage — 5-minute decision

  1. Read title — RTL, DV, FPGA, or PD?
  2. Highlight must-haves (usually first 5 bullets)
  3. Count matches from course map (Section 4) — need ≥3
  4. Check for red flags (Section 3)
  5. If pass: tailor resume + apply within 48 hours
Analysis paralysis

Spending 2 hours researching a junior role you'll never apply to is procrastination. Triage in 5 minutes, apply or skip, move on. Volume + quality portfolio beats perfect JD analysis.

🎯 What to remember

  • Three families: ASIC RTL, FPGA, IP/SoC integration — same foundations, different emphasis.
  • Apply when you match ~60% of must-haves; ignore most nice-to-haves.
  • Map every JD keyword to a course module + artifact.
  • Use honest resume bullets — I2C capstone is your strongest line.
  • Lesson 12.4 covers what to learn next (STA, UVM, PD).
