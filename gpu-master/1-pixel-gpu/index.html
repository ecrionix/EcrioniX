💡 1. What is a Pixel?

Foundations

The word Pixel is a portmanteau of Picture Element (PIX + EL). It is the smallest controllable unit of color and light that can be rendered on a computer display, smartphone screen, or digital television.

When you look at a photograph or 3D video game, you are not viewing continuous analog paint. Instead, your screen is a physical grid of millions of microscopic square pixels placed edge-to-edge. When viewed from a standard distance, the human visual system fuses these microscopic squares into a seamless, high-resolution image.

Underneath every LCD or OLED screen, each pixel is manufactured from three microscopic sub-pixel emitters: Red, Green, and Blue. By controlling the electrical intensity of each sub-pixel emitter with digital hardware circuits, the GPU produces any perceived color in the human visible spectrum.

🎨 2. RGB Colour Representation & Binary Mathematics

Color Theory

Digital graphics use the Additive Light Color Model. Unlike paint pigments (which absorb light subtractively), digital sub-pixels emit light rays. When Red, Green, and Blue light beams combine at full intensity, they produce pure white light. When turned off completely, they produce black.

🏗️ 4. Hardware Block Diagram & Register Pipeline

Architecture

In real graphics hardware, signals do not jump instantly to the display. They pass through synchronous digital flip-flops clocked by a high-frequency system oscillator (e.g. 50 MHz). This registered pipeline stage prevents timing glitches and allows the GPU to run at GHz clock speeds.

🎓 9. What We Learned

Summary

1. Additive RGB Model

Colors on electronic displays combine through additive light rays. Red + Green makes Yellow; Red + Blue makes Magenta; Red + Green + Blue makes White.

2. 24-Bit Truecolor Quantization

Each color channel uses 8 bits (256 discrete integer levels). 3 channels × 8 bits = 24 bits total, allowing 16.7 million distinct visual colors.

3. Synchronous D-FF Registers

Outputs are held stable by clocked flip-flops. Inputs are latched only on the rising clock edge (posedge clk), introducing exactly 1 cycle of pipeline latency.

4. Valid Strobe Handshaking

When pixel_valid_in is LOW, downstream graphics hardware ignores the output bus, preserving previous pixel values and saving dynamic power.
