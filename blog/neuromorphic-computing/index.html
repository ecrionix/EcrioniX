What Is Neuromorphic Computing?

Neuromorphic computing builds processors that mimic the structure and behavior of biological neurons and synapses. Instead of a clock‑driven pipeline, these chips operate event‑driven—they only consume power when a spike (an electrical impulse) occurs. This architecture delivers ultra‑low latency and dramatically lower energy use compared to traditional GPUs or CPUs.

Why It Matters

  • Power‑efficiency: Current research reports 100 µW per million synapses, which is 10‑30× lower than conventional AI accelerators.
  • Real‑time perception: Near‑instantaneous response makes it ideal for drones, AR/VR headsets, wearables, and autonomous vehicles.
  • On‑chip learning: Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) can adapt directly on the device, removing expensive cloud round‑trips.

Current Milestones

Several industry leaders have already shipped or announced neuromorphic chips:

  • Intel Loihi 2 – 130 M spikes/s, on‑chip Spike‑Timing‑Dependent Plasticity (STDP) for continual learning.
  • IBM TrueNorth – 1 million programmable neurons, used in NASA rover vision demos.
  • Google “SNN‑TPU” prototype – announced 2024, promising 100× speed‑up for event‑camera workloads.

What’s Coming Next?

Rumors suggest commercial neuromorphic edge‑AI chips will appear in flagship smartphones by 2027. Expect tighter integration with 5G, low‑power always‑on assistants, and privacy‑preserving on‑device AI.
