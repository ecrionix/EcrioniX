💡 Simple Explanation

The gate controls whether a conductive path forms between the source and drain, acting like an electrical valve handle.

🔬 Device Physics

In an NMOS transistor, applying a sufficiently positive gate-to-source voltage (VGS ≥ VTH) induces a vertical electric field across the gate dielectric, creating an n-type electron inversion channel.

Material: Polysilicon / Metal
Role: Controls channel conductivity
Current (I): Near-zero DC leakage (IG ≈ 0)

How a MOSFET Works

The fundamental principle of field-effect switching in three steps:

1

Apply Voltage to the Gate

The gate electrode is isolated from the silicon substrate by an ultra-thin insulating dielectric (gate oxide). Applying a positive voltage (VGS) establishes a vertical electric field across this dielectric.

2

Inversion Channel Forms

When VGS exceeds the threshold voltage (VTH), the electric field repels positive holes into the substrate and attracts mobile electrons to the surface, creating an n-type inversion layer connecting Source and Drain.

3

Current Flows Under Drain Bias

With the conductive electron bridge active, applying a voltage between Drain and Source (VDS) causes electrons to drift across the channel, sustaining conventional drain current (ID) from Drain to Source.

Educational & Scientific Note: This interactive 3D model illustrates simplified enhancement-mode NMOS operation. The threshold voltage (VTH = 0.7 V) is an illustrative textbook value chosen for clarity; real-world transistors exhibit threshold voltages determined by gate dielectric thickness, work function engineering, channel doping, and operating temperature (e.g. 0.2 V – 0.4 V in modern FinFET/GAA technologies). This tool is a conceptual learning model and does not compute quantitative SPICE current-voltage equations.
