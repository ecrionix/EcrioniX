4-Bit Carry-Select Adder

Combinational Design · combinational, arithmetic, architecture

Speed up addition by precomputing the upper half twice — once assuming carry-in 0, once assuming carry-in 1 — then muxing in whichever guess turned out right, instead of waiting for the carry to ripple through.

Module Specification
Write a synthesizable Verilog module named top_module matching the ports below exactly.
📐 Circuit Schematic Diagram
⚡ Timing & Synthesizability Constraints

Timing Contract: Strict synchronous timing aligned to posedge clk or zero-delay combinational propagation.

RTL Constraints: Synthesizable Verilog-2001. No # delays in synthesizable RTL.

Concept: A carry-select adder splits the word into blocks; each upper block computes its sum twice in parallel (once for cin=0, once for cin=1) while the lower block's real carry is still being resolved, then a mux instantly picks the correct precomputed result once that carry arrives — trading duplicated hardware for a shorter critical path than a full ripple-carry chain. The whole trick lives in the mux select: sum[3:2] = c1 ? sum1_hi : sum0_hi;. Inverting that select (~c1 instead of c1) silently swaps in the wrong precomputed half every single time — it still compiles and still looks like a mux-based adder, it just always guesses backwards.

Problem Statement & Requirements

      
Speed up addition by precomputing the upper half twice — once assuming carry-in 0, once assuming carry-in 1 — then muxing in whichever guess turned out right, instead of waiting for the carry to ripple through.

      
Hardware design teams at leading semiconductor firms evaluate these exact port contracts, race-condition assertions, and corner cases during technical design reviews and ASIC engineer interviews.

      
      
Concept: A carry-select adder splits the word into blocks; each upper block computes its sum twice in parallel (once for cin=0, once for cin=1) while the lower block's real carry is still being resolved, then a mux instantly picks the correct precomputed result once that carry arrives — trading duplicated hardware for a shorter critical path than a full ripple-carry chain. The whole trick lives in the mux select: sum[3:2] = c1 ? sum1_hi : sum0_hi;. Inverting that select (~c1 instead of c1) silently swaps in the wrong precomputed half every single time — it still compiles and still looks like a mux-based adder, it just always guesses backwards.
